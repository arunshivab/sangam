Confluence

Two streams enter from opposite sides, bend toward each other and leave as a single stem — the literal sangam, drawn with the restraint of a wayfinding glyph rather than an illustration. The two inbound strokes carry different colours (ink and river-teal) so that "two becoming one" survives even when the mark is 16 px; in single colour the geometry still reads because the junction is a clean visual weld, not an overlap. It doubles as a lowercase-y / arrow-down, which quietly suggests "sign in here" at the top of a login screen.

S / I monogram

An S built from two half-round hooks, split apart and threaded onto a single vertical I. Read one way it is the initials SI; read another it is two counterparts clasped onto a shared spine — which is exactly what a federated identity layer does. The construction is strictly one stroke weight and one radius, so it engraves, embroiders and reverses out cleanly; the hooks and the spine can be coloured separately to hint that the partners differ while the backbone does not. Strong app-icon candidate because the vertical spine gives it a confident centre of gravity in a square.

Wordmark with a joint

No symbol at all: a tightly tracked grotesque wordmark in which a short accent bar sits between "Sangam" and "ID" — a coupler, not a separator, so the name reads as one object made of two parts. The reduced mark is simply the capital S with that same bar docked at its waist, which keeps favicon and wordmark provably from the same drawing. This is the least decorative and most infrastructural option; it ages well, translates instantly into a Devanagari companion wordmark, and never competes with partner brands sitting next to it on a consent screen.

सं — the first syllable

The Devanagari syllable सं, drawn as the mark itself, with the anusvāra lifted out as a free accent dot in warm ochre. The move is deliberately understated: a reader of Devanagari sees the name begin; everyone else sees a calm, well-balanced glyph with a floating dot, which is also the shape of a status indicator. It carries cultural warmth without a single ornament, and the detached dot becomes a reusable brand atom (session active, verified, consent granted) across the product. Needs a custom-drawn, outlined letterform before shipping so the counters stay open at 16 px.

Yantra, reduced

A yantra stripped to its three structural moves: enclosing circle, inscribed square turned on point, and the bindu at centre. Sacred geometry is a diagram of ordered relationships, which is a surprisingly literal description of an identity system — many participants arranged around one trusted centre. Kept to a single stroke weight and a solid centre dot, it stays legible as a favicon where a real yantra would collapse into mud, and it is the most obviously Indian of the six without borrowing a single decorative motif. Pairs with a lighter, wider-tracked wordmark to avoid feeling like a badge.

Palm leaf

A single palm-leaf folio seen flat: the rounded leaf edge, two incised lines of text, and the cord hole that binds one leaf to the next. That hole is the whole idea — a record is only useful when it can be threaded to every other record, which is the argument for a shared identity layer. It is the warmest of the six and the most obviously kin to LiPi, and because the silhouette is a horizontal pill with one strong dot, it holds together at 16 px better than any of the other figurative options. Best paired with a serif wordmark; it looks wrong next to a hard grotesque.
