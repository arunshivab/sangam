WHY IT LOOKS LIKE THIS
Partner identity is a chip above the card and a 3 px left rule — it colours nothing structural. Swap LiPi for Aarogya and the form is pixel-identical, so a phishing clone can't hide behind a partner skin.
The Sangam wordmark sits above the partner chip. The user is on our domain; the partner is context, not host.
Turnstile is a passive strip, not a widget slot: invisible verification still gets a visible, non-shifting line of reassurance. Failure swaps the same strip to an interactive challenge — same height, no reflow.
400 px column, 320 px floor, 16 px inputs (below that iOS zooms on focus).

NOTES · TRUST MOMENT
The arrow points from the partner to Sangam and is labelled REQUESTS — the partner is asking, Sangam is holding. Reversing it would imply data flowing outward by default.
The "will not be shared" column is given equal visual weight. Most consent screens omit it; it is the single strongest trust signal on the page.
Real values are shown next to each claim ("Ravi Menon"), so consent is to specific data, not to a category.
Allow is visually dominant but Cancel is a full 48 px button, never a text link — declining must not feel like an error path.
Liability is stated in plain words: after approval, the partner's policy governs.
